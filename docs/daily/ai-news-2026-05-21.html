Podcast Deep Dive

AI-Native ITSM: How Serval Rebuilds Enterprise IT From Scratch

Guest: Jake Stauch (Serval Founder & CEO) | Host: Training Data. The first-principles philosophy behind Serval, the AI-native enterprise service management platform.
Rebuilding ITSM from First Principles
Core insight: workflows on top of databases is the right abstraction, but manual building/maintenance is too slow. Serval keeps these primitives but uses AI to build workflows and update databases. "Describe the workflow in natural language - it generates code immediately, zero development time." Same for databases - describe what data from which sources, system auto-generates code and keeps it synced.
"Building Automation Must Be Easier Than Doing It Manually"
Jake's key insight: when faced with "manually reset a password" vs "open a workflow builder and drag to build," IT people choose manual. "If it were actually easier to build the automation, you would build the automation. People in the moment have to make that decision, and you want that decision to be very, very easy to opt for the automation."
Dual Agent Architecture: Admin Agent x Helpdesk Agent
Serval splits agents into two layers: Admin Agent helps IT admins build tools/skills (published after approval & permissions); Helpdesk Agent serves end users, can only use admin-approved tools. "The helpdesk agent can use its full reasoning ability to solve user problems, but can only use tools IT has expressly said are okay." This resolves the core enterprise AI tension: individuals want autonomy, organizations want control.
Model Selection Engineering Reality
OpenAI models excel at user interaction (calling correct tools, appropriate responses); Anthropic models excel at automation (code generation). Model upgrades are not plug-and-play - each new model brings tradeoffs. Sometimes they downgrade: "This model might not be as smart, but we know it's going to behave the right way." Customer insight > product advantage - because "product is copyable overnight."
AI-Native Organization Practices
Serval eliminated Solutions Engineering and SDR roles - AEs use Serval for instant answers and battle cards. "Fewer, better" is the hiring mantra. AI gets "right of first refusal" on every role - assume it can be automated first. "People remain the biggest moat you can have. Having better people in the room is really the only thing that will keep you ahead of the competition."

Why This Matters
Karpathy x Anthropic: Talent Flow Signals Gravity Shift
Karpathy joining Anthropic isn't just personnel news. He was OpenAI founding team & Tesla AI Director - his choice signals a subtle shift in where the LLM frontier's center of gravity lies. "The next few years will be especially formative" suggests he believes Anthropic is positioned to define the next paradigm. 131K likes shows the entire industry is watching this talent flow.
Tokenmaxxing: AI's Economic Model is Taking Shape
Sam Altman's capacity commitments + YC $2M token investment + "Tokenmaxxing confirmed" paints a clear picture: the AI industry is moving from "pay-per-use" to "compute futures." Fortune 500 CIOs debated token costs as their #1 topic (Aaron Levie). When compute becomes scarce, the ability to commit long-term capacity itself becomes a competitive moat.
AI-Native IT: Dual Agent Architecture Solves Autonomy vs Control
Serval's Admin Agent + Helpdesk Agent design elegantly resolves Jake's identified tension: individuals want autonomy, organizations want control. This maps directly to the core friction in enterprise AI adoption today - it's not that models lack capability, it's that organizations don't know how to safely unleash it. Jake's conclusion: saying "yes" by default puts companies ahead, even with security risks - "those risks are real, but the leaders will learn the most."
Model Upgrades Are Not Plug-and-Play
Jake's experience echoes Anthropic's postmortem: new model releases are not plug-and-play. Prompt tuning and infrastructure built around known quirks break with new models. Sometimes they downgrade back - because reliability matters more than raw intelligence. This reinforces what Anthropic's Caitlin said: the same model on different harnesses yields wildly different eval results. Harness engineering is becoming a deeper moat than the model itself.
One Question a Day: When Karpathy chooses Anthropic, OpenAI launches compute futures, Google ships 24/7 personal agents, and Fortune 500 CIOs debate token costs at dinner - the AI industry is shifting from "who can train the best model" to "who can turn models into the best products and services." The winners in this transition may not be those with the strongest models, but those who best understand how to unleash model capability within constraints. Serval's dual-agent architecture, Anthropic's harness engineering, OpenAI's capacity commitments - all answer the same question: when model capability is no longer the bottleneck, what is?
